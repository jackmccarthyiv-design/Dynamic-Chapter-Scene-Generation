Chapter 1: The Crossing

The pass opened before her at dawn, a narrow throat of stone between two walls of ice. Wind came down from the heights, carrying with it the scent of snow and distance. She had been walking since midnight, when the moon still hung low over the eastern peaks, but now the sky was brightening to a pale, uncertain gray.

The cairns were still there—stone piled upon stone by travelers who had come before. Some ancient, some recent. She added her own rock to the nearest one, feeling the cold bite into her bare hand. The gesture felt both necessary and futile, like so many things did now.

Below her, the valley spread out in shades of sage and shadow. Mist clung to the lower slopes, reluctant to burn away even as morning arrived. Somewhere down there was the town she'd left behind. Somewhere up here, beyond the pass, was whatever came next.

She pulled her coat tighter and kept walking. The path narrowed, then widened, then narrowed again. The stone underfoot was worn smooth by centuries of boots and hooves and the patient work of wind. To her left, the cliff dropped away into nothing. To her right, the mountain rose like a wall, streaked with ice and shadow.

A hawk circled overhead, riding the thermals that rose from the valley. She watched it for a moment—its lazy spirals, its absolute freedom—then looked away. There was still a long way to go before the pass crested and she could see what lay on the other side.

The wind picked up, tugging at her hair, her coat, her resolve. But she didn't stop. One foot in front of the other. The cairns watching. The mountains indifferent.

By noon she would reach the summit. By evening she would be in a different world entirely.

But for now, she was here—suspended between what she'd been and what she might become, in a place where the only sound was wind and the only company was stone.
